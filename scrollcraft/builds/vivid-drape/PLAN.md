# Plan: grammar, gate, score

## Grammar: Chaptered editorial (uniqueness.md §2.2)

Why it wins: the user chose "Editorial", and the substance is a method (colour analysis)
plus a founder story, which is exactly what this grammar fits.

Why the other seven lost:
- **Filmic one-shot:** no single linear argument that wants hidden seams; a drape session is discrete trials.
- **Live surface:** she sells a service, not software.
- **Continuous world:** no real geography; and it needs video/worldflight (no video budget).
- **Typographic poster:** the whole point is seeing colour on a face, which needs a photograph.
- **Gallery / catalog:** the six services are not a collection of objects to browse.
- **Split stage:** no two-sided argument to resolve.
- **Rhythmic cutlist:** an energy-brand grammar; wrong for a calm, trust-led consultation.

Grammar constraints honoured: no fixed bar (a folio in the margin instead, with a Contents
index), a type-only title page (no photograph above the fold), hard cuts between chapter
grounds (no drift), no centred hero copy, media sits in its own column, no `scrub`,
`spotlight`, or `magnet`, the close is a colophon plate with the CTA as running text.
(The drape stage is a pinned *image* act, not a pinned type crossfade: its copy is static,
only a state readout updates.)

## Signature move: "The drape"

Scroll lays seasonal drapes (a grey cape, then Winter, Summer, Autumn, Spring) under a
real portrait. Each drape wipes up from the bottom like fabric being laid on, and its
colour reflects softly onto her skin. The page's accent regrades live with every drape.
The visitor picks the one they kept looking at; that choice regrades the rest of the page
(testimonial ground, closing plate) and is written into the booking email's subject.
